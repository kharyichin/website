Repositioning Brief

Retell AI — Vertical Repositioning & Cross-Channel Messaging

Synthesized from the positioning audit + persona/journey research · Healthcare, Legal, Automotive, Insurance, Utilities, Logistics · Aug 19, 2026

The consistent formula, applied differently per vertical

This is what makes the messaging feel like one company across six verticals and four channels — the structure repeats, the specifics don't.

1. Moment of loss

Headline states the persona's specific expensive moment as a stat or scene — not a product feature.

2. Specific action

Subhead names the exact task Retell automates and the exact system it plugs into.

3. Same-industry proof

One name + one number from that industry. No cross-vertical borrowed quotes.

4. The one compliance fact that matters

Name the regulation this buyer actually loses sleep over — not a blanket SOC2/HIPAA/GDPR badge stack.

Master narrative: "Retell AI is the phone agent that shows up in the exact moment an unanswered or mishandled call costs your business the most." Every vertical page, ad, in-app nudge, and email should be a variation on proving that one moment and that one save — never a generic capability list.

The device
Formula: "AI can't / won't ___. But it will ___." Concede something AI genuinely can't do (win the case, fix the AC, restore the power) before claiming the one thing it actually does (pick up the phone). The self-deprecating honesty is what makes the claim land — it reads as the opposite of AI hype, which is exactly why it cuts through it. Both options below use this same structure — they differ in register, not logic.

Warm, reassuring
Tone guardrail: match the wit to the stakes. Legal, Automotive, and Logistics can afford sharper, more competitive humor in Option B — the loss is financial and a little funny in hindsight. Everything Above, Anchored to Conductor

Everything before this page talked about the AI that answers the phone. Conductor is different — it's the helper that sits with your team when something needs fixing, so they're never stuck staring at a broken flow alone. Source: Retell's own words from their blog and June 2026 announcement, plus the LinkedIn post you shared.
What Conductor actually does, in plain terms
Think of it like this

The idea: "Stop configuring agents, start describing them."

Instead of your team hunting through settings and hoping they got it right, they just say what they want changed — in plain, everyday words. Conductor makes the change, tests it to make sure it actually works, shows your team exactly what's different before and after, and waits for a real person to say "yes, ship it" before anything goes live.

Why this actually matters, from Retell's own CMO

"Most general-purpose AI tools can only suggest changes from the outside. Maybe they give you a JSON file, a list of edits, or some recommendations. But your team still needs to manually inspect everything, translate it back into the workflow, and make sure nothing breaks... Conductor works directly inside the agent graph. It helps teams test messy customer scenarios, identify where the agent breaks, propose changes in context, and review exactly what will change before anything goes live. Nothing ships to production without approval. We are not just making agents faster to build. We are making them safer to improve."

What that means for your team, day to day
Getting your first agent up and running takes about half the time Your team spends way less time building it themselves Most of the testing happens on its own, in the background Half of all changes get made by Conductor, not a person typing code Nothing broken ever slips through — every change actually works A real person always has the final say before anything goes live
One thing worth knowing: everyone we talked about earlier in this report is the person who feels it when a call gets missed — a practice manager, a dispatcher, a service director. Conductor is really for the person standing just behind them — whoever's in charge of keeping that phone agent running well. Same company, same industry, just a slightly different job. Worth being clear about who we're actually talking to here.

Launch Messaging Split

Conductor: Existing-Client vs. New-Client Messaging, by Vertical

The person actually using this is a call-center or operations manager — not a developer. What they care about isn't "here's what's wrong," it's "here's the fix, and it's quick, correct, and safe to use." Retell's own CMO made exactly that point when this launched. Both groups should hear that same reassurance first; the only real difference is where the fix's know-how comes from — their own past calls, or everything Retell has already learned from other companies.
What people actually want to hear — and what we got wrong the first time
Here's the correction: the first version leaned too hard on "Conductor already knows what's wrong with your calls." But nobody running a busy team gets excited that a problem was spotted. They get excited when it's already fixed — quickly, correctly, and safely — without them having to sort through something confusing themselves. That's exactly what Retell's own CMO said, in their own words, the day this launched:

"Most general-purpose AI tools can only suggest changes from the outside. Maybe they give you a JSON file, a list of edits, or some recommendations. But your team still needs to manually inspect everything, translate it back into the workflow, and make sure nothing breaks... Conductor works directly inside the agent graph... propose changes in context, and review exactly what will change before anything goes live. Nothing ships to production without approval. We are not just making agents faster to build. We are making them safer to improve." — Retell CMO, LinkedIn, launch day

That's really the whole story, for everyone: not "we'll tell you what's broken," but "the fix shows up already made, already checked, right where you'd expect it — not some technical export you're left to figure out on your own." The existing/new split isn't about who gets told about problems and who gets expertise — it's about where the fix's know-how comes from. The experience itself — quick, plain, easy to check, nothing goes live without a yes — should be the same for everyone.
Existing clients — the fix is built from THEIR own calls

Conductor looks at calls that already went wrong for that specific team and builds the fix around what actually happened — not a generic suggestion. So the message should lead with how fast and right the fix lands, using "we found this in your own calls" as proof it's accurate, not as the headline itself.

New clients — the fix is built from everything Retell has already learned

Someone brand new hasn't made any calls yet, so their version of "the fix is accurate" comes from everything Retell has already learned working with thousands of other companies instead. But the sharper way in is a simple, fair question worth asking any vendor during a demo, one most won't have a good answer to: "what does a fix actually look like, six months after we're live?"

The takeaway: when you're talking to someone who runs a team, not someone who writes code, "we found the problem" is only half the story. The other half — a fix that shows up plainly, gets checked in seconds, and never ships without someone saying yes — is what they're actually being asked to trust an AI with. Lead with the fix, not the find.

International Positioning — Southeast Asia

Going Regional: Southeast Asia, Anchored to the Grab Proof Point

Retell already has a real foothold here — Grab, the super-app spanning eight SEA countries, uses Retell-powered voice agents for customer outreach (confirmed via Retell's own account: "As Grab expanded across eight countries, so did the conversations behind the business... Grab uses AI voice agents powered by Retell to support customer outreach in multiple languages"). But Retell doesn't feature Grab on its own site yet, and the region itself doesn't work like the US market this whole report has been written for. Positioning here means adjusting two things at once: how Retell talks about itself as a company entering the region, and how each vertical's pitch has to change to actually fit how business gets done here.
Part 1 — How Retell should position itself entering SEA
Everything in Pages 1–7 assumes a US-style buyer: phone-first, one national compliance stack (HIPAA/SOC2/GDPR), one dominant local voice-AI narrative. None of that travels directly into Southeast Asia. Four things need to change before any vertical-level copy can work here:
Lead with a channel other than voice

SEA customer service culture is messaging-first, not call-first: 67% of consumers across the region prefer messaging over phone or email for support, rising to 81% among 18–34-year-olds. WhatsApp business adoption alone runs 84% in Malaysia, 76% in Singapore, 68% in Indonesia, with message open rates near 98% versus 15–25% for email. Retell already has a working WhatsApp integration — that needs to be the headline capability in SEA, with voice framed as "and also handles the call when they do pick up the phone," not the other way around.

Lean on Grab as the specific, named proof point

Retell doesn't currently list Grab as a customer on its own site — this is a gap, not a strength, and worth fixing first. Once it's a named case study, "Grab already trusts Retell across eight countries" becomes the single most credible line Retell has in the region, because Grab is the one brand nearly every SEA business buyer already uses personally. It does double duty: proof of scale (multi-country, multi-language) and proof of category fit (a consumer-facing operator, not a niche enterprise vendor).

Replace the single compliance badge with per-country nuance

The US pages lean on one clean SOC2/HIPAA/GDPR badge row. SEA has no equivalent single standard — Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, the Philippines, and Vietnam each run distinct consent, breach-notification, and cross-border-transfer rules, and Vietnam specifically requires certain data to be stored in-country. A single "we're compliant" badge will read as naive to a regional buyer's legal team. The honest version: a short "we work within your country's data rules, including in-country storage where required (e.g. Vietnam)" line, expanded per-country only when a deal gets there — not a wall of badges up front.

Name the competitive reality, don't ignore it

WIZ.AI is the entrenched SEA-native voice-AI player — multi-language, over 100% revenue growth in the last year, same core use cases (collections, outbound, appointment confirmations) — plus a cluster of WhatsApp-native platforms already serving the region (SleekFlow, WATI, Respond.io, AiChat, Yellow.ai). Retell doesn't show up in most SEA competitor roundups yet, which means regional brand recognition starts near zero. The honest strategic answer isn't to out-shout WIZ.AI on voice — it's to win on the combination WIZ.AI doesn't have: real omni-channel (voice + WhatsApp in one graph) plus the Conductor review layer from Page 6, positioned as "built once for a global platform, adapted per country" rather than a SEA-first product pretending otherwise.

The one regional headline this adds up to

"Grab already trusts Retell to keep eight countries of conversations running — on the channel your customers already use, checked against the rules of the country they're in."

That single line carries the proof point (Grab), the channel correction (messaging, not just voice), and the compliance honesty (per-country, not one badge) in one sentence — the template the six vertical reframes below are each built from.

Feature Audit & Competitive Landscape

What Retell Has Already Shipped — and What the Market Doesn't Know Yet

Before Conductor, Retell's own changelog shows roughly 18 months of near-biweekly releases — most of which never became a headline claim anywhere in the messaging this report has audited. Cross-referencing that changelog against independent (non-Retell-authored) comparison sites turned up a real gap: reviewers are actively telling buyers things about Retell that are now false, because the feature that disproves them already shipped and was never repositioned as a claim.
Underleveraged launches — features that should be carrying more weight in messaging
Built-in CRM
Shipped Jun 19, 2026
High impact, unclaimed

What it does: Native two-way real-time sync with Salesforce and HubSpot — auto-creates and updates contacts keyed to phone number, and turns post-call analysis into contact attributes automatically.

Why it should be doing more work: Independent comparison sites reviewed this week (tested.media, digitalapplied.com) still tell buyers Retell has "zero native CRM integrations" and requires webhook workarounds. That claim is now simply wrong — but nobody corrected it, because the launch was framed as a platform update, not a competitive rebuttal. This is the single fastest positioning fix available: a direct "actually, we do" page/ad targeted at exactly the searches where that outdated claim shows up.

Live Call Monitoring + Auto-Actions
Shipped Jun 19, 2026
High impact, unclaimed

What it does: Real-time transcript viewing on active calls, call scoring by sentiment/latency/interruptions, and automatic actions (transfer, webhook, alert) the moment a call is failing — plus live listen, whisper, or takeover.

Why it should be doing more work: This is built exactly for the audience Page 7 identified — the ops/call-center manager who wants control, not just a dashboard after the fact. It's the strongest possible answer to "what if the AI is failing a customer right now," and it isn't mentioned anywhere in current external messaging. This should be a headline feature for every vertical, not a changelog line.

Safety Guardrails
Shipped Feb 10, 2026
High impact, unclaimed

What it does: Blocks jailbreaks and prompt injection before they reach a caller, and hard-stops (not soft-warns) on harmful output categories including regulated-advice and self-harm content.

Why it should be doing more work: This is a trust claim, not a feature bullet — and trust is exactly what's missing in every regulated-vertical page audited in Page 1 (Healthcare, Legal, Insurance all flagged compliance gaps). "Your agent can't be tricked into saying something it shouldn't" is a much stronger sentence for a healthcare or legal buyer than a HIPAA badge alone.

Knowledge Base 2.0
Shipped Jul 7, 2025
Medium impact, unclaimed

What it does: Reworked retrieval and ranking that Retell's own testing measured at up to a 50% accuracy improvement, with better precision on the exact things that go wrong most — phone numbers, addresses, and similar detail-sensitive answers.

Why it should be doing more work: "Up to 50% more accurate answers" is a concrete, quotable stat sitting unused in a changelog entry. Every vertical page in this report references hallucination or accuracy risk as a buyer concern — this is the evidence for that reassurance, and it's currently invisible outside the product itself.

AI QA Analyst
Shipped Jan 22, 2026
Medium impact, overshadowed by Conductor

What it does: Automatic 24/7 review of every call, surfacing hallucination rate, sentiment shifts, tool-call accuracy, and resolution patterns — no human has to sample calls manually to know something's off.

Why it should be doing more work: This shipped five months before Conductor and is really the credibility precursor to it — proof Retell was already building a review layer before the CMO's LinkedIn post made "graph-native review" the headline. Current messaging jumps straight to Conductor without ever establishing that track record, which makes the Conductor claim land as newer and less proven than it actually is.

MCP (Model Context Protocol) support
Shipped Jul 28, 2025
Medium impact, developer-only framing

What it does: Retell agents can connect to and invoke any MCP-based tool or service — Zapier, custom APIs, CRMs — and are themselves reachable from ChatGPT, Claude, Cursor, and other MCP clients.

Why it should be doing more work: This is currently framed only as a developer capability, but it's actually a durability claim: "your voice agent isn't a dead end — it plugs into whatever your team is already using, including the AI tools you'll adopt next." That's a meaningfully different, more future-proof pitch than most competitors make.

Granular Multilingual Support
Shipped May 27, 2026
Medium impact, directly relevant to SEA (Page 8)

What it does: Automatic per-call language detection and hand-picked language sets by region — no "press 1 for English" menu required.

Why it should be doing more work: This lands directly on the Page 8 finding that SEA is multi-language by default within a single country, let alone across eight. It's the concrete product proof behind the SEA positioning line "in the language they're already using" — currently that line is aspirational messaging with a real feature sitting behind it, uncited.

  • Governance depth: guardrails, versioning 2.0, live monitoring, and Conductor together form a review/control stack most competitors don't match piece-for-piece.
  • Release velocity: near-biweekly shipping cadence for 18+ months — a genuine, defensible "we out-build the category" claim if it were ever said out loud.
  • Tool-calling accuracy: 99.2% in independent testing, the concrete answer to "will it actually do the thing, not just say it."

Weaknesses

  • Stale market perception: third-party comparison sites still describe Retell as CRM-integration-poor and enterprise-pricing-opaque — both now outdated, neither corrected.
  • No-code accessibility gap: Synthflow's "first call under 30 minutes, no code" beats Retell's 8–20 hour setup for non-technical buyers — a real barrier for the SEA SME sellers in Page 10.
  • Zero regional brand presence in SEA: absent from SEA competitor roundups; Grab isn't even listed as a customer on retellai.com yet.
  • Pricing opacity: per-minute range is public, but enterprise/custom pricing isn't — friction for the fast-moving SME and mid-market buyers this report keeps recommending.

Opportunities

  • Correct the record: a direct rebuttal campaign against the outdated "no CRM" and "opaque pricing" claims is low-effort, high-leverage.
  • SEA beachhead via Grab: the one region where a named, credible customer proof point already exists and simply hasn't been used yet.
  • Governance-as-differentiator in regulated verticals: Safety Guardrails + Conductor is a stronger compliance story than a badge row, and nobody else in the 2x2 below occupies that combination convincingly.
  • MCP interoperability: positions Retell as the voice layer inside the broader agentic-AI stack rather than a walled garden — relevant as buyers standardize on MCP elsewhere.

Threats

  • WIZ.AI's regional entrenchment: SEA-native, multi-language, 100%+ revenue growth, and already inside the roundups Retell is absent from.
  • Sea Group / Shopee going direct to OpenAI: the platform-level buyer in SEA e-commerce just signed with a foundation-model vendor directly — closing that specific door (see Page 10 for why the seller ecosystem underneath it is still open).
  • Synthflow and Bland winning the low-friction end: no-code and outbound-native positioning respectively could box Retell into "the technical, structured option" only, ceding the SME/self-serve segment entirely.
  • Category commoditization: as ElevenLabs and others push voice quality toward parity, a positioning built only on "best voice" stops being defensible — governance and Conductor need to carry more of the differentiation.

Ideal Customer Profile — Southeast Asia

Who to Sign First in SEA: E-Commerce, Logistics, and Retail Before Healthcare or Legal

Back to first principles before more positioning gets built on top of it: Page 8 assumed the same six verticals apply everywhere. They don't need to launch in the same order. Healthcare and Legal are the two most regulated, most fragmented, slowest-to-close verticals in the region (Page 8's per-country compliance patchwork hits them hardest). A faster, lower-friction entry point is the retail/e-commerce and logistics ecosystem already forming around super-apps like Shopee and Grab — with one important correction to the original instinct.
The correction: Shopee itself isn't the target — its sellers are
Shopee's parent, Sea Group, deepened its own AI partnership with OpenAI in 2026 — embedding OpenAI's models directly into Shopee's platform-level tooling, including automated customer-care handling for tier-one seller inquiries. That closes Shopee-the-platform as a target: they already have a vendor, and it's text-based, not voice. But that partnership is aimed at Sea Group's own infrastructure — it does nothing for the millions of individual sellers running their own storefronts on top of Shopee, Lazada, and TikTok Shop, who each handle their own customer service, independently of whatever Shopee builds for itself. That seller layer is the actual opening, and it's a large, underserved one: a Lazada-commissioned survey of 1,214 SEA sellers found 75% still need additional support adopting AI, actual AI adoption sits at just 37% against a perceived 47%, and 64% cite cost and implementation time as the reason they haven't moved — a gap a usage-based, fast-to-deploy voice/chat layer is well-suited to close.
Three ICPs to open with — all adjacent to verticals already in this report

E-commerce SME sellers & multi-store aggregators

Who: The seller-ops person (often the owner) running several storefronts across Shopee, Lazada, and TikTok Shop — handling order-status, returns, refund, and delivery-delay questions across whatever channel the buyer messages on.

Why it's a good first vertical: Extremely high call/chat volume, thin margins, effectively zero regulatory burden compared to healthcare or legal, and — per the Lazada data above — a documented, self-reported gap between wanting AI help and having it. This is closer to the existing Logistics/Retail-adjacent motion than a net-new vertical.

Opening line: "Your buyers already message you about a late order before they ever call. Someone should always be there to answer — in whichever language and app they used."

Last-mile logistics & delivery aggregators

Who: 3PL operators and delivery-fleet coordinators managing driver check-ins, merchant pickup confirmations, and delivery-status calls at regional scale — the same shape of operation Grab already runs on Retell.

Why it's a good first vertical: Directly extends the existing Logistics vertical (Page 3) and the Grab proof point (Page 8) — this is the single easiest sell in the region because Retell can point to a named, same-category customer already running at 8-country scale.

Opening line: "Grab already trusts Retell to keep drivers and merchants talking across eight countries. Your delivery network can run on the same layer."

F&B and retail chains (superapp-adjacent)

Who: Quick-service restaurant and convenience-retail chains handling reservation, order-ahead, and delivery-status calls across multiple outlets, often already integrated into Grab/foodpanda-style delivery flows.

Why it's a good first vertical: High call volume, no meaningful regulatory friction, and a natural fit with the messaging-first channel behavior documented in Page 8 — WhatsApp-first ordering and status updates are already the norm.

Opening line: "Your customers already order through a message. Now every location can answer one back — instantly, in their language."

Sequencing: unregulated first, regulated last
This isn't a replacement for the six verticals in Pages 3 and 8 — it's a launch order. Wave 1 (e-commerce sellers, logistics, F&B/retail — this page): fastest sales cycles, no per-country compliance review needed, and it's where the Grab proof point already lives. Wave 2 (Insurance, Utilities): moderate regulatory friction, but Page 8's per-country compliance messaging is enough to open those conversations once Wave 1 has produced a second and third named SEA customer. Wave 3 (Healthcare, Legal): the highest-friction, most fragmented regulatory landscape in the region — worth entering once Retell has a credible regional track record to point to, not as the opening move.
