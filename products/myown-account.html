MyOwn Account

Singapore's first digital bank account built for kids aged 7–15. I owned the go-to-market roadmap that took it from a regulatory grey area to the bank's fastest-growing product, with 80,000+ accounts opened in the first 6 months.

OCBC · Global Consumer Financial Services · Launched Oct 2024 Product page →

The Brief

MyOwn Account is a full bank account in a child's own name, for kids aged 7 to 15 — not a joint account co-owned with a parent or legal guardian. It runs as a kid-facing view inside the existing OCBC Digital app: the child gets their own debit card and can pay, scan-and-pay, and PayNow independently, while a parent gets a linked view with real-time visibility, spend limits, and override control.

The case for it was a whitespace, not a hunch. Our youth product lineup had a real gap in it.

Why this gap mattered: the government-mandated CDA/Baby Bonus accounts only cover specific child expenses, and MightySavers stops well short of a debit card. Kids in the 13–15 band were increasingly going out with friends unsupervised into a retail environment that had already gone cashless, with nothing built for them to learn real financial decisions in — only a jump straight to FRANK at 16.

Objections We Had to Answer

Singapore is a conservative market on money and parenting, and this wasn't a toy account — it was a real bank account for a 7-year-old. These are the objections that shaped the product and the launch narrative the most, in the conversations we had with parents and regulators.

Without physical cash in hand, how will my child actually learn to budget?

→ Guardrails, not a blank card — parents set the spend limits and see every transaction in real time through the linked view, and the in-app financial literacy content teaches budgeting deliberately rather than assuming it's picked up by osmosis.

My child is online constantly already — what happens the first time someone tries to scam them?

→ Safety built into the product, not bolted on — Money Lock and Kill Switch give an immediate way to freeze the account, and the financial literacy program dedicates content specifically to online safety and scam awareness, taught at the age the risk actually starts.

Is my child even mature enough to be trusted with their own bank account?

→ Training wheels, not a blank check — the parent-and-child dual view means every transaction is visible to the parent, limits are parent-set, and passwords can be reset by the parent, so independence comes with supervision built in from day one, not removed.

Positioning & the Big Idea

The core idea: this shouldn't be a sub-card tucked inside a parent's account, it should be the child's own version of the real OCBC Digital app — same brand, a kid-appropriate experience, with the guardrails visible rather than hidden. That's what let us frame it as teaching kids what a real bank relationship looks like early, rather than just handing them spending power.

The tagline moved from an early, more aspirational direction — "Unlock your child's financial superpowers" — to what's live today: "Help your child get smarter about money."

The Disney partnership did double duty on positioning. Kids needed a reason to actually want their own account; parents needed reassurance that the "fun" wrapper wasn't coming at the expense of substance. Instead of Disney only supplying card artwork, their characters (Ralph & Vanellope from Wreck-It Ralph) became the vehicle for the financial literacy content itself — comics and activity sheets on saving, spending, and digital safety, distributed both digitally in-app and in print to 110 secondary schools. The partnership carried the emotional hook and the parent-reassurance job at the same time.

Customer Journey

The parent is the decision-maker and the account opener; the child is the end user. The journey has to work for both, which is why the "Trust Test" stage carries the most weight.

Persona

A parent of an 11-year-old — comfortable banking online herself, but hasn't yet decided whether her child needs their own account and debit card.

Scenario

Her child now goes out with friends after school in a cashless retail environment, and keeps asking for their own way to pay — she wants independence for her child without losing visibility into what they're spending on.

Launch Roadmap

Pre-launch
Book-building and teaser content, PR agency and reporter outreach, and warm outreach to existing OCBC customers with kids in the target age range. Pre-registration opened 3 Sep 2024.
Soft launch — 15 Oct 2024
Account opening enabled in the OCBC Digital app for existing OCBC customers, ahead of the public event.
Official launch — 20 Oct 2024
Launch event at the OCBC Wisma Atria branch — Ms Sun Xueling, Group CEO Helen Wong, Disney character meet-and-greets, full paid and earned media push across OOH, performance, and PR.
Post-launch
CVP refinement, MGM and referral pushes, continued positioning iteration, and trendjacking moments (PSLE results day, Secondary 1 orientation) timed to when parents are already thinking about their child's next stage of independence.

Results

80,000+ New customers within the first 6 months, making it the bank's fastest-growing segment

Constraints We Navigated

This is a live, regulated product at a licensed bank, not a prototype — every guardrail (spend limits, the parent-and-child dual view, Money Lock, Kill Switch) had to clear compliance and risk review before a single card could ship. That shaped the launch timeline as much as any creative decision did.
